How to Invoice as a Freelancer: A Simple Checklist

Getting paid on time starts with a clear invoice. Here is what every freelance invoice should have.

What to include

  • Your name or business name and contact details
  • Client name and billing address
  • A unique invoice number
  • Issue date and due date
  • A line for each service: description, quantity, rate
  • Total, currency and tax if it applies
  • How to pay you

Tips that help you get paid

Send the invoice the day the work is approved. Set a due date (7 to 14 days is common). Write the payment method clearly. Follow up politely one day after the due date.

Tools

A spreadsheet is enough to start. As you grow, dedicated invoicing software can send reminders and take card payments. Compare the fees before you commit.

Expense Tracking for Freelancers: Keep It Simple

You do not need accounting software on day one. You need a habit.

Four categories are enough to start

  • Software and subscriptions
  • Equipment
  • Marketing
  • Fees (payment, platform)

The weekly habit

Once a week, spend 10 minutes: log each expense with date, category and amount, and keep the receipt. At month end compare income against expenses to see your real profit.

Why it matters

Many freelancers feel they earn more than they do because platform fees and tools add up. Seeing net profit each month helps you set better prices.

How Freelancers Can Price Their Work

Pricing is the hardest part of freelancing. This is a simple method to get a starting number.

Step 1: Set a monthly income goal

Add your living costs, taxes you expect to owe, and tool costs.

Step 2: Estimate billable hours

Most freelancers cannot bill every hour. Selling and admin take time. Plan on a share of your week as billable, not all of it.

Step 3: Divide

Monthly goal divided by billable hours gives your minimum hourly rate. Quote project prices above that, based on the value to the client, not just your hours.

Freelance Taxes: Set Aside Money From Every Payment

Employees have tax taken out of each paycheck before they see it. Freelancers do not. If you spend everything you earn, tax season becomes a crisis. The fix is a habit, not a spreadsheet.

A simple rule

Every time a client pays you, move a fixed share into a separate savings account the same day. Many freelancers start with 25 to 30 percent of each payment. The right number depends on your country and your income, so treat this as a starting point and confirm it with a tax professional where you live.

Why a separate account

Money you cannot see is money you will not spend. A separate account turns a vague intention into a system, and you always know how much of your balance is actually yours to use.

Keep records as you go

Save your invoices and receipts sorted by month. When it is time to file, everything is already in one place. A local tax professional can tell you exactly what you owe and which expenses you can deduct.
